1. WHAT IS BIDMAGNET?
---------------------
Every year Spanish public bodies (councils, ministries, hospitals,
universities...) buy billions of euros of IT services through public
tenders ("licitaciones"). Small and mid-sized IT companies often miss
out because:
  - tenders are scattered and found too late,
  - it's hard to tell which ones are worth bidding for,
  - the paperwork is long and one missing document gets a bid thrown out,
  - nobody knows what price will win.

BidMagnet is a "virtual bid team" for these companies. A company fills in
its profile once. BidMagnet then shows the tenders that fit it, explains
why, and for the ones it chooses, prepares the groundwork: a checklist of
every required document (each pointing to the exact page of the official
papers), how the bid is scored, and what price is realistic.
The company still submits on the official government platform itself.


2. HOW IT WORKS (in plain words)
--------------------------------
  1. COLLECT    Every day we download all new public tenders from the
                national platform (PLACSP) and keep the IT ones.
  2. UNDERSTAND We read the key facts (budget, deadline, buyer, who won
                before) and, for chosen tenders, the 100+ page documents,
                turning them into a checklist with page references.
  3. MATCH      For each company we score every open tender from 0 to 100
                and list the reasons ("in your region", "needs a
                certificate you don't have", "only 5 days left").
  4. PREPARE    Checklist, scoring rules, price formula, warnings, and
                history of the buyer and competitors.

  Example: a 25-person software firm in Valencia opens BidMagnet and sees
  "85/100 – Cemetery management software, La Vall d'Uixó, 3,704 EUR,
  18 days left. Warning: needs ENS security certification."

4. KEY NUMBERS (January–September 2026)
---------------------------------------
  - 247,038 public tenders published (national + regional platforms);
    23,059 are IT (3,524 in Catalonia, 1,441 in the Basque Country).
  - Today (19 Oct 2026): 23,470 IT tenders in the database, 494 open.
  - 3,882 different companies won IT contracts; the biggest are
    Telefónica, Inetum, Indra, Orange and Vodafone.
  - 4,588 IT contracts are expected to end in the next 12 months
    (chances to replace the current supplier).
  - Usually only 2 companies bid for an IT contract (median).
  - In competitive tenders, winners bid about 10–14% below the budget.
  - 90% of awards show who won; 99% show how many bids came in.
  - Database size: see the storage note in section 7.
  - Example of full bid results: an AI assistant tender in Aragón drew 19
    bids; 9 were thrown out (3 put price info in the wrong envelope, 4
    scored too low technically, 2 priced too low). The winner bid 35%
    below budget. In Castellón, 20 bids; the top 3 were within 0.64
    points; Telefónica came 9th, Deloitte was excluded.
  - More bid-result lessons (Valencia/Murcia/Alicante):
      * Windows licences: won with SECOND-HAND licences at less than half
        the price of 13 resellers of new ones.
      * 329k EUR smart-platform contract: a local SME won bidding 45% below
        budget; Telefónica (2% below) came 8th.
      * Often everyone maxes the non-price criteria, so price decides.
      * One company (Península Corporate Innovation) appears in 4 of these
        tenders: excluded twice, runner-up twice.
  - "Similar tenders" for the cemetery software: other councils' cemetery
    contracts were mostly won by the same vendor, with 1 bidder and no
    discount: incumbents keep their software contracts.


5. THE THREE DEMO TENDERS
-------------------------
  - Cemetery software, La Vall d'Uixó (3.7k EUR): easy entry, but its two
    official documents contradict each other on the security level.
  - AI chatbot, Canary Islands Government (50.7k EUR): price barely

  - Cybersecurity, Aguas de Lorca (142k EUR, 3 lots): heavy certification
    requirements; bidding for all 3 lots gets you excluded.

7. STORAGE NOTE (Supabase free tier = 500 MB)
---------------------------------------------
  Now: 180 MB (about a third of the limit) for 23,470 IT tenders
  (January 2026 to today, national + regional), including:
    tender documents (links)   59 MB
    tenders                    44 MB
    AI fingerprints            23 MB  (for matching by meaning)
    requirements/criteria/results/bids/stats   the rest
  Earlier: 154 MB for only 8,688 tenders, before we removed a duplicate
  copy of every tender and stopped rewriting unchanged ones (each tender
  now ~7 KB instead of ~18 KB).
  Room for roughly another year of data on the free tier. The PDFs
  themselves are NOT stored (only links); that would use Supabase Storage
  (1 GB free), which is separate.


8. FOR THE TEAM: HOW THE BACKEND WORKS (technical)
--------------------------------------------------
  Everything is in the repo under data-pipeline/. Full how-to: its README.md;
  database guide for the app: API.md; app guide: APP_GUIDE.md; AI/dev notes: implementation.txt.

  8a. Architecture
    PLACSP (government XML feed)
        |  Python scripts (laptop now; GitHub Actions daily at 05:30 UTC)
        v
    Supabase Postgres (EU, Ireland)  <-- Next.js web app (web/) reads it with
                                        supabase-js; signed-in users only
                                        AI features (summaries, chat, drafts)
                                        run in the app's server routes (OpenAI)
    Diagrams: architecture-simple.png (slides), architecture.png (detailed)

  8b. Python scripts (data-pipeline/)
    placsp_fetch.py    downloads monthly zips / live feed pages / tender PDFs.
                       Handles the government's own TLS certificate (FNMT)
                       and its slow, flaky server (retries).
    placsp_parse.py    turns each XML entry into a clean record; keeps only
                       the newest version of each tender; tags IT tenders by
                       sector code (CPV 72*, 48* = software/IT services;
                       302* hardware; 3242*/6421* telecom).
    build_dataset.py   filters IT, writes out/tenders.jsonl + CSVs + stats.md.
    load_supabase.py   uploads to Supabase in batches; only rewrites tenders
                       that changed; refreshes the statistics views.
    extract_tender.py  defines the checklist format and checks every page
                       reference against the PDF ("verified").
    bid_results.py     checks the page references of full bid results and
                       links bidders to their tax ID (NIF) from the
                       platform's opening reports or past awards.
    embed.py           computes AI fingerprints for new/changed tenders and
                       company profiles (free model, runs on a laptop or in
                       the daily GitHub job; no API key).

  8c. Database tables (Supabase)
    tenders               one row per tender (title, buyer, budget, deadline,
                          region, sector codes, status, search index)
    tender_criteria       how bids are scored (e.g. price 70, quality 30)
    tender_requirements   solvency / certificates / classification asked for
    tender_documents      links to every official PDF (terms, specs, award
                          minutes, evaluation reports...)
    tender_results        who won, how many bids, lowest/highest bid, amount
    tender_extractions    the cited checklists (JSON)
    tender_bids           EVERY bidder on a tender: price, scores, rank,
                          why excluded (from award reports; page-cited)
    tender_bid_reports    summary + insights per tender's bid results
    tender_embeddings     AI "fingerprint" of each tender title (384 numbers,
                          multilingual model) for matching by meaning
    companies             customer profiles (each user sees only their own)
    tender_decisions      Go / No-go / Watch per company and tender
    company_documents     the company's own uploaded documents (ROLECE, ENS,
                          ISO, insurance...) + extracted details; files in
                          the private storage bucket "company-documents"

  8d. Backend features (SQL in backend.sql)
    match_tenders(company)  fit score 0-100 + reasons for every open tender
    search_tenders(text)    Spanish-aware search box
    buyer_stats             per buyer: typical bidders, discount, top winners
    competitor_stats        per winning company: wins, value, regions, buyers
    upcoming_renewals       contracts ending in the next 12 months + incumbent
    bidder_history          how each company bids across tenders, including
                            the ones it lost (avg rank, discount, exclusions)
    similar_tenders(id)     past tenders most similar in meaning: winner,
                            discount, number of bidders
    partner_candidates(id)  companies that won similar work (same region,
                            big enough wins) to bid with
    Security: row-level security; logged-out visitors see nothing; users
    only see their own company profiles and decisions.

  8e. How to run it (needs data-pipeline/.env with DATABASE_URL)
    python3 placsp_fetch.py month 202609            # download a month
    python3 build_dataset.py raw/*.zip --segments all
    uv run --with "psycopg[binary]" load_supabase.py   # --force after code changes
    New database? Run schema.sql then backend.sql in the Supabase SQL editor.

  8f. Data & backend summary (Oct 2026)
    - The full collection pipeline above, run daily since 2 October 2026.
    - The Supabase database, security rules and all backend features.
    - 3 demo checklists with 116 verified page references.
    - Storage optimisation (each tender ~7 KB instead of ~18 KB).
    - API.md so the frontend can build every screen.
    - Full bid results from award reports (all bidders, prices, scores,
      exclusion reasons): the data no public source provides in structured
      form; 9 tenders / 115 bids so far, all page-verified.
    - The logic behind Bid prep: price simulator (tender formulas, legal
      abnormally-low rule, rivals from past discounts) and the proposal
      outline / AI draft.

  8g. Known limits
    - Regional tenders carry less detail than national ones (fewer
      document links and criteria in the feed).
    - Some award reports are scanned or have broken fonts; those bid
      results can't be read without page images.
    - Certificate requirements are only detected where the XML or a
      checklist mentions them; most details are inside the PDFs.
    - Checklists for new tenders need the paid Claude API (or are written
      by hand with Claude Code, as for the 3 demo tenders).
    - A few buyers' discount figures look odd (unit-price contracts).
